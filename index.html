Core Mechanism: Reflection Coefficient (σ)

A higher reflection coefficient means the agent is less permeable across the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB), creating a more potent and reliable osmotic gradient. This is key to drawing water out of the brain.

0.9

Mannitol

Can leak across a damaged BBB, risking rebound ICP.

1.0

Hypertonic Saline

Highly impermeable, ensuring a stable osmotic gradient.

Patient Suitability: The Consensus

Evidence and physiological principles strongly favour HTS in the majority of acute scenarios encountered in the ED. Mannitol remains an alternative where HTS is contraindicated.

Impact on Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPP)

CPP is the net pressure gradient causing cerebral blood flow to the brain. HTS actively supports CPP by raising Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP), while Mannitol can compromise it via diuresis-induced hypotension.

Efficacy: ICP Reduction Over Time

While both agents lower ICP rapidly, evidence suggests HTS provides a more durable reduction. Mannitol carries a risk of rebound ICP as it eventually leaks across a damaged BBB, reversing the osmotic gradient.

Safety Profile: Key Risks

The safety concerns for each agent are distinct. Mannitol's primary risks are haemodynamic and renal, while HTS requires careful metabolic monitoring.

❤️ Caution in severe CHF

Risk of ODS with HTS is exceedingly low in normonatremic patients and is associated with rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia.

Mannitol: Admin Checklist

  • ✅
    Dose: 0.25-1 g/kg (20% solution)
  • 🔬
    Filter: MUST use an in-line 5-micron filter.
  • ❄️
    Crystals: Inspect and warm to re-dissolve if present.
  • 💧
    Monitor: Serum osmolality (<320 mOsm/L), renal function, and fluid balance. Prepare for diuresis.

HTS: Admin Checklist

  • ✅
    Dose: 2-5 ml/kg (3% or 2.7% solution)
  • 💉
    Control: MUST administer via a syringe driver. NEVER free-flow.
  • 🏥
    Route: Central line preferred, can use large bore peripheral line in an emergency.
  • 🧂
    Monitor: Serum sodium (Target 145-155, alert >160 mmol/L), acid-base status.
